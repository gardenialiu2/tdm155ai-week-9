### sample-context-repo/piece/description.md
---
title: Alabaster — what it is
author: Jo Lindqvist (invented; sample)
---

Three broadsides, 11 by 17, Caslon, printed on the Vandercook in the Bow and Arrow Press basement, edition of 12. Sheet one: the 1859 version of "Safe in their Alabaster Chambers." Sheet two: the 1861 version, with the second stanza Emily sent to Sue after Sue objected. Sheet three: Sue's sentence, alone, small, in the middle of the page: "I am not suited dear Emily with the second verse."

The film: 90 seconds, top-down, one frame per sort. The second stanza of 1859 is locked in the chase. Hands take it apart letter by letter and set the 1861 stanza in its place. No voiceover. Room tone from the press room and the sound of type on the stone.

What I wanted: to make the variant physical. In the manuscripts a variant is a little cross and a word in the margin. On a press, changing a word means pulling actual metal out and putting other metal in, and the two stanzas don't take the same amount of space, so the whole form shifts. Sharon Cameron says Dickinson "chose not to choose" between variants. I wanted to see what it costs to choose.

Where it stands: printed and filmed. I'd reprint sheet three; the impression is too heavy.

### sample-context-repo/process/prompts/claude-2026-10-19-variant-script.md
---
title: Prompt and script — word-by-word diff of the two stanzas
date: 2026-10-19
author: prompt by Jo; script by Claude; edited by Jo
---

**My prompt:** "Here are two stanzas of a poem (below). Write me a tiny Python script that prints which words are removed and which are added going from the first to the second, and counts letters in each, because I'm going to set them in metal type and need to know how many sorts change."

**What came back** (I changed the tokenizer to keep the dashes, since dashes are sorts too):

```python
import re, sys
a = open(sys.argv[1]).read(); b = open(sys.argv[2]).read()
tok = lambda s: re.findall(r"[A-Za-z']+|–", s)
A, B = tok(a), tok(b)
print("out:", len("".join(A)), "sorts;", "in:", len("".join(B)), "sorts")
print("removed:", [w for w in A if w not in B])
print("added:", [w for w in B if w not in A])
```

**Output on the second stanzas:** out: 212 sorts, in: 247 sorts (letters and dashes, not spaces). Removed: nearly everything. Added: nearly everything. Which is the point: the second stanza isn't a revision, it's a replacement. That became the film's structure.

Sharon Cameron's *Choosing Not Choosing* (University of Chicago Press, 1992) argues that Emily Dickinson's fascicles, the hand-sewn booklets into which she copied her poems, should be read as deliberate compositions rather than as storage. Cameron focuses on the variant words Dickinson recorded in the margins and at the ends of poems, marked with small crosses. Editors from Mabel Loomis Todd and Thomas Wentworth Higginson through Thomas H. Johnson treated these as unresolved drafts and chose among them; Cameron argues that the refusal to choose is itself the poem's mode. She extends this to the fascicle as a unit, suggesting that poems placed together in a fascicle read one another, so that the boundary of "the poem" is also left open. The book's central example chapters address Fascicles 15 and 16 among others, and the argument has been influential on later manuscript-centered scholarship, including the work of Marta Werner and the digital editions.

## Sharon Cameron, *Choosing Not Choosing: Dickinson's Fascicles* (1992)

The book about variants. Her argument, as I understand it: the variant words in the fascicles aren't drafts waiting for a decision, they're part of the poem; Dickinson keeps the alternatives open on purpose. ### sample-context-repo/piece/description.md
---
title: Alabaster — what it is
author: Jo Lindqvist (invented; sample)
---

Three broadsides, 11 by 17, Caslon, printed on the Vandercook in the Bow and Arrow Press basement, edition of 12. Sheet one: the 1859 version of "Safe in their Alabaster Chambers." Sheet two: the 1861 version, with the second stanza Emily sent to Sue after Sue objected. Sheet three: Sue's sentence, alone, small, in the middle of the page: "I am not suited dear Emily with the second verse."

The film: 90 seconds, top-down, one frame per sort. The second stanza of 1859 is locked in the chase. Hands take it apart letter by letter and set the 1861 stanza in its place. No voiceover. Room tone from the press room and the sound of type on the stone.

What I wanted: to make the variant physical. In the manuscripts a variant is a little cross and a word in the margin. On a press, changing a word means pulling actual metal out and putting other metal in, and the two stanzas don't take the same amount of space, so the whole form shifts. Sharon Cameron says Dickinson "chose not to choose" between variants. I wanted to see what it costs to choose.

Where it stands: printed and filmed. I'd reprint sheet three; the impression is too heavy.
