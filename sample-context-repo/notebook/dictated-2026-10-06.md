Okay so the thing I keep coming back to is the letterpress class I took sophomore year and how slow it is to change one word. You have to unlock the chase, pull the line, find the sorts, and if the new word is longer the whole line has to be respaced. And then I'm reading the Dickinson for the other class and the editor's note says she left alternative words with little crosses and never picked. And I thought: what if you had to pick. Physically.

So the piece is, I think, two versions of one poem printed, and a film of the change between them. The poem has to be one where the variant is big, not one word, a whole stanza. The Alabaster one, the one where Sue wrote back and said she didn't like the second verse. That's a real editorial fight, in 1861, between two people who lived next door to each other.

Things I don't know yet. Whether the Bow and Arrow has enough Caslon in one size to set both stanzas at once, because I want to film the change, so both have to exist at the same time. Whether I film it top down or from the side. Whether there's a voiceover. I think no voiceover. I think the sound is just the type.
