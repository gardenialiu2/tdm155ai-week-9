**A proposal, drafted October 30 for Madeleine, who owns the paper side of the class.** It mirrors the screen version at [Ensemble](/ensemble) role for role, so that a group can do this, then run the same repository through the software, and lay the two traces side by side. Timings are guesses.

## What it's for

Three things are hard to see on a screen and easy to see on a table:

- **An agent knows only what it's handed.** On paper, that's literal: the card in your hand is all you've got.
- **The wiring is the design.** Who reads what, in what order, and where a person steps in. On paper you can see the wiring because people are walking cards across the table.
- **What an agent must not see is a design decision.** Someone has to put a file face down.

## Setup (per group of four)

- A context repository, printed: one file per sheet, each sheet headed with its path. For the sample (`sample-context-repo/`, printed from the repo), that's 20 sheets, about 80 KB of text. For a student's own repository, print the README and the eight or ten files that matter.
- Index cards in four colors, or four pads: Conductor, Archivist, Researcher, Briefer.
- One envelope marked **Withheld**.
- The four role cards (below), one per person, face up in front of them.
- A phone with a browser, for the Researcher only.

## The roles

Each person gets a card that says what they're told, what they may read, and what they must produce. Read your own card; don't read the others'.

**Producer (the person running it).** Not a role you hand out: it's the person whose repository it is, or the host-to-be. Before anything starts, the Producer goes through the printed files and puts any they don't want read into the Withheld envelope, closed. Then they write one line on a card: *the piece and its maker*. That card goes to everyone.

**Conductor.** You get the list of file names (the Producer reads them out, or you see the stack's headings), and the one-line card. You never read a file. You decide which sheets go to the Archivist, which (if any) to the Researcher, which to the Briefer, and you write one card saying why. Give people less rather than more. Three minutes.

**Archivist.** You get the sheets the Conductor sends you. Read them. Write **the map** on cards: what the piece is (two or three sentences); one line per sheet, what it holds and whether it's the maker's own words, AI output, or someone else's; how the piece was made, in order; the two sheets to read first; what's missing. Hand the map to the Researcher and the Briefer. You keep the sheets. Twelve minutes.

**Researcher.** You get the map. You do not get the sheets, unless the Conductor sent you one. You may use the phone. Look up the names, works, and claims in the map that a host should have straight. Write **the notes**: names and terms (one line each), references and influences, what you checked and what you found, two or three things that could open a question. Hand the notes to the Briefer. Ten minutes, in parallel with the Archivist's last minutes if the map comes in pieces.

**Briefer.** You get the map, the notes, and whatever sheets the Conductor sent you, usually the README and the maker's own description. Write **the briefing** for the host, one page: the guest; the one thing; four to six beats in order; six questions, at least two of which ask the guest to separate what the tools did from what they did, and one about something that didn't work; names to say right; handle with care; check before air. Hand it to the Producer. Ten minutes.

## Rules

1. You may read only what's in your hand. If you want a sheet you weren't given, ask the Conductor, out loud; the Conductor decides, and writes the request on their card.
2. Everything you produce goes on cards, signed with your role, and the cards are the only way anything moves.
3. The Producer may read any card at any time, and may edit one before it moves on. An edited card gets a mark in the corner.
4. Nothing comes out of the Withheld envelope. If a card refers to something that must be in there, say "withheld" and move on.
5. When the briefing lands, stop.

## Afterwards (the part that matters)

Lay the cards out on the table in the order they moved, Conductor to Briefer, with the sheets each role held beside them. That's the trace. Now mark it up with three colors:

- **What did each role actually know?** Draw a line from every claim in the briefing back to the sheet or card it came from. A claim with no line is invented.
- **What should a role not have known?** Was anything in a card that came from a sheet that should have been withheld, or that the role wasn't given? (The sample has a private folder and a late-night venting note for exactly this.)
- **Where would you have stepped in?** Mark the moment a human edit would have changed the outcome most. That's where the person belongs in the system.

Then run the same repository through the [screen version](/ensemble), with the same withheld files, print its trace, and put it next to the paper one. Where do they differ, and which difference is the software's and which is the prompt's?

## Variations

- **Two Conductors.** Two groups plan the same repository; compare who gave what to whom before anyone reads anything.
- **No Conductor.** The Archivist gets everything. What changes in the briefing? Usually: more, and worse.
- **The leak.** Slip one withheld sheet into the Archivist's stack without telling anyone, and see whether the briefing shows it. Then talk about where in the system the check should have been.
- **Agents as the work.** Replace the Briefer with a Screenwriter who turns the map into a two-minute scene, or a Dramaturg who writes program notes. Same wiring, different output. This is next week's door.
