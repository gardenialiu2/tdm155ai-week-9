# Libraries: casts and formats

Two small libraries of cards for building a theater of agents. [Ensembles](ensembles.md) says who is in the room: Friends, Seinfeld, the Olympian gods, the nine Muses, the Beatles, and the Backstreet Boys, each with distinct voices, relationships, competing wants, and what makes the group interesting together. [Formats and mechanics](formats-and-mechanics.md) says what rules they play under: the roles of a podcast or radio show (host, guest, sidekick, producer) and the machinery of reality television (challenges, confessionals, votes, eliminations, alliances, producer interventions).

## Mix them

The libraries are meant to be crossed. Two experiments come out of one table:

- **Same cast, different rules.** Keep the persona prompts fixed and change the format. The Seinfeld four in a podcast, then on *Survivor*, then in a writers' room. What stays the same is character; what changes is what the rules bring out.
- **Same rules, different cast.** Keep the format fixed and change the cast. One elimination format run with the Olympians, then the Muses, then Friends. What changes is the cast; what stays the same is what the format does to any group.

Students could, for example, put the Greek gods through a reality-show format, or have a sitcom ensemble prepare a podcast. Keep the run small (a cap on turns, a shared event log, a confessional channel) so the difference between two runs is something you can read.

## How the cards are written

Each character card has a voice, wants, relationships, a two-sentence persona seed to paste into a system prompt, and sources. Each mechanic card has what it is, one rule an agent can follow, what it produces, what usually goes wrong with LLM agents, and sources.

The Beatles and the Backstreet Boys are real people. Their cards describe **public personas**, as presented in films, press conferences, interviews, credits, and the bands' own marketing, and attribute each description to where it came from. They make no claims about anyone's private personality or private life. A persona prompt for a real musician plays the stage character, not the person.

The fictional and mythological casts come from the shows and the ancient texts. Where a familiar trait is a later addition (most of the Muses' specific domains, for example, are Roman-era assignments, not Hesiod's), the card says so.
