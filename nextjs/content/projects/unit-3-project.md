**TDM 155AI · Unit 3 · Week 9, November 4 · The Final Project is 40% of your grade**

In Unit 2 you made one piece of media with AI's help: your Unit 2 media project. In Unit 3 you level that up. AI lets you work with many assistants at once, so you can make more than any one person could make alone: not one piece but a system of media, a system that produces media, a series, an installation, or an interactive game.

Reminder: your **First Project** (the finished piece and your context repository) is due **this Friday, November 6**. The podcast interviews follow the week after.

## What the Final Project can be

Any project that takes your use of AI, your media production skills, or both further than your Unit 2 media project, in ways we'll be learning about throughout Unit 3. For example:

- **a system of media**: many pieces that belong together and play off each other
- **a system that produces media**: agents that make a podcast series, a channel, a show, episode after episode
- **a series**: your Unit 2 media project, now as the first of many
- **an installation**: work that lives in a room, with lights, projections, graphics, screens, sound, cameras, and people walking through it
- **an interactive game**: a mystery, a world, a show the audience plays
- **a theater of agents**: a cast of characters you design, running as the work itself

Start from two questions: **what will your system produce, and how and where will someone encounter it?**

You will not be graded on the quality of your code. You'll be graded on what you make and on how well you can explain it.

## What you hand in

Everything goes in your **final portfolio, due Friday, December 11, at 5pm**.

Your portfolio is a **Google Drive folder and/or a GitHub repository**, whichever suits your work. At a bare minimum, it contains:

1. **Your work**: your weekly work, your Unit 2 media project, and your Final Project.
2. **Your code**: everything you built, including your agents' definitions (their instructions, characters, and roles) and the runs and logs worth keeping.
3. **Links to your media**. All of your media should also appear in your Slack channels.
4. **Your final solo interview** (see below).

We'll give you a **template** for the portfolio. You're free to add to it and reshape it during the final weeks of the term: references, research, prompts and chats, drafts and failed attempts, credits, anything that shows how you made your work. A README at the top that says what's there and where to start will help a lot.

**Your final solo interview.** We'll schedule a final solo interview for each of you, and it goes in your portfolio too. More details to come.

**Have it complete and accessible by your interview.** In the interview you'll walk us through your portfolio, so it needs to be finished and shared with us by then. You can keep revising it until **5pm on Friday, December 11**, and the version you stand behind at that point is the one that counts. Say what you changed.

## How it's assessed

As in Unit 2, we look at both the work and your account of making it: how creative and ambitious it is, the decisions you made, and whether you can explain what your system does, why it's built that way, and what you and the tools each contributed. A result you can't explain or change doesn't show what you learned.
